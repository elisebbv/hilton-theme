dear you,

Nine years ago, I dropped a four-year Bachelor's degree program in psychology with no plan. I just knew I didn't want to be a therapist like I'd originally thought. The upside, looking back, is this was almost certainly the right call for me. The downside? I traded a future of $150/hour rates for the famously underpaid title of Fiction Author.

It wasn't long after dropping psychology that I took a one-year creative writing certificate program and finally finished the first draft of a novel I'd been working on. That was the year I learned about the path to getting published. It looked something like:

1. Write a book
2. Get rejected by every publishing professional you ever talk to 
3. Rinse + repeat until someone caves (e.g., a literary agent, or YOU when you decide to self publish instead, or give up altogether).

I saw this and, again, after coming out of that sure-fire psychologist salary pipeline (because let's be honest, I'd have ACED being atherapist), my reaction wasn't to go crawling back to the psych program properly cowed. I took one look at that future and said, "Yep. I've found my people."

And after eight years of following steps one through three, it finally happened. A literary agent said YES! (After initially saying no, providing helpful feedback, and offering to reconsider if I wanted to revise the project. Naturally, I rewrote the whole thing on the off- chance of a maybe. I told you: I found my people- we're the neurotic ones.)

I've been dreaming of sharing this news for the last eight years. I hope to be riding this high for the NEXT eight years as well (though, fortunately or unfortunately, I do know that's not how this works).

All that to say: 8 years, 3 books, and 150 rejections from publishing professionals later, I am officially represented by [Olivia Emerick](https://manuscriptwishlist.com/mswl-post/olivia-emerick/) of the [Metamorphosis Literary Agency](https://www.metamorphosisliteraryagency.com/about)!

I'll be sharing more about what that means and what comes next in future letters, but for now I'm off to celebrate like I won the lottery! (Minus the irresponsible spending of as-yet fictional cash.) If you'd like an excuse for a little treat, get yourself something in my honour the next time you're out and about! I'd love to celebrate this milestone with you!

yours,  
elise
